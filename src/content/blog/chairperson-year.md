I spent three years with the **IEEE-CIET Student Branch**: first as an organising executive, then as WIE Vice Chairperson, and finally as **Chairperson for 2025–26**. The last year taught me the most.

### Leading people who don't report to you

Everyone on a student team is a volunteer with exams, internships and their own plans. You can't order anyone to do anything. What worked for me was clear ownership: every event had one lead, every task had one name next to it, and wins were shared publicly.

### Every rupee needs a reason

Running 10+ events and 3+ hackathons meant managing budgets and sponsors. Sponsors want to know what they get in return, so I learned to pitch reach, audience and visibility, which was my first real taste of business development.

### Use data, not memory

We tracked registrations, attendance and feedback in Google Sheets for events reaching **1,000+ students**. That data showed which time slots worked, where people dropped off between registering and attending, and which formats got the best feedback. Planning got better once we stopped relying on gut feeling.

### Thinking bigger

The branch grew to **500+ active members** and 20+ events a year. Events like **Hackfest'25**, a national-level hackathon with SAP Labs India and Coding Blocks, showed what a student team can pull off with the right partners. My year as Chair opened with **Triggered** and **Infraverse** in August 2025.

> **Takeaway:** being Chairperson felt like running a small company: people, money, customers and results, with a new team every year.
