When I joined **Recrivio** as a GTM intern, I expected to learn sales. What I actually learned is that go-to-market is a research and systems problem first, and that suited the way I was trained to think as an engineer.

### Start with who, not what

My first weeks went into ideal customer profile (ICP) work. Before anyone writes an outreach email, someone has to answer: which companies feel this pain the most, and who inside them decides? Recrivio builds **RecriAuth** for background verification, so I studied how companies of different sizes and industries hire, how often they verify candidates, and what slows them down.

### Research 120+ companies, find the patterns

I researched and analysed over 120 companies. Treated as a dataset rather than a list, they sorted into a few clear segments:

- **High-volume hirers** who feel every day of delay and care most about turnaround time.
- **Regulated sectors** where verification is non-negotiable and compliance drives the decision.
- **Fast-growing startups** that skip checks to hire quickly and carry the risk without realising it.

Each segment needs a different message. Speed matters to the first, audit trails to the second, and the cost of a bad hire to the third.

### The real competitor is "we'll verify later"

The biggest surprise: the main competitor often isn't another vendor. It's companies skipping background verification because it feels slow. A skipped check saves a few days, but a bad hire costs months of offboarding, rehiring and retraining. Positioning became about removing the trade-off between speed and safety.

### A few months in

Recrivio was featured in The Indian Express on exactly this problem, and in November I was recognised with the **Rising Star award**. The note on it now sits on my desk as a reminder to keep learning.

> **Takeaway:** debugging and GTM work the same way. Break the market into parts, find where buyers get stuck, and fix the message for that exact point.
